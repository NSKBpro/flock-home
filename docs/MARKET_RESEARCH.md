# Market research (October 2026)

Why this game, and what the realistic outcome looks like. Figures are from the sources listed at the end; most are third-party estimates, not audited numbers.

## Where the growth is

- **Hybrid-casual puzzle** (simple core mechanic plus progression, mixed ads and purchases) was the only casual segment to grow in-app revenue in 2025, up 20% to $4.2B. Sort, Screw and Block puzzles lead it.
- **Pixel Flow** (conveyor + colour sort) went from launch in August 2025 to about $550K a day at peak. Analysts credit the visible holding tray: players manage it actively, so failure feels like their own mistake and paying to continue feels fair.
- **Arrow puzzles** (tap a piece, it leaves if the path is clear) are the newest wave. About 1,600 arrow games appeared on Google Play since July 2025; two of them took 63% of all installs, and 7 in 10 never reached 10,000 installs.
- Analysts expect the next winners to **recombine** proven elements rather than copy one game or invent something alien.

**Conclusion:** don't clone a current hit (saturated, and portals reject clones). Combine the arrow rule with a Pixel Flow–style visible holding area. That is Flock Home.

## Web portals

- **Poki** reports about 100M monthly players; **CrazyGames** about 50M, concentrated in English-speaking Tier 1 markets. Both are company claims.
- 62% of web players have downloaded or bought a game after first discovering it on the web (Poki survey, June 2026).
- CrazyGames: free to publish, no exclusivity, human QA, two-stage launch (Basic Launch to measure engagement, then Full Launch with ads). Developer share is not published in the main docs; 2026 jam terms list 60% of ad revenue and 70% of purchases.
- CrazyGames screens explicitly for clones, reskins and asset flips, and rejects games that "look like a prototype".

## Money: realistic expectations

- A well-performing casual game on a major portal typically earns **$200–2,000 a month** from revenue share. The top of Poki's catalogue reaches about €1M a year. The median is close to zero.
- Web rewarded video eCPM in mid-2026: about $3.60 global, $6–7 in Tier 1 markets.
- Players prefer rewarded ads to interstitials about 4 to 1. Showing interstitials too often raises churn.

## Risks

- Most games earn little. Plan for a cheap test, not a big bet.
- Mobile success at scale depends on paid user acquisition, which a solo developer cannot fund. Mobile only makes sense after the web numbers prove retention, or through a publisher.
- The mechanic can be copied quickly once visible. Speed of iteration matters more than secrecy.
